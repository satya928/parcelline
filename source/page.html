Questions

Is a PID the same as a land identification number?
Yes. PID is the name BC, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and PEI use for the number that identifies one parcel of land. Alberta calls it a LINC and Ontario calls it a PIN. The number stays with the land when it is sold. It only changes when the lot is split or merged.
Is the PID the same as my title number or tax roll number?
No. The title number identifies the ownership record and changes on every sale. The assessment roll number is used for property tax. Both often point to the same lot, but ParcelLine searches by PID.
How accurate is the boundary?
Boundaries come from each province's parcel map and are usually within a metre or two. They are not a legal survey. For exact bearings and distances, order the registered survey plan from the land titles office.
Where does the municipality come from?
From the government's legal boundary map: ParcelMap BC and the BC municipal boundary layer, New Brunswick's Local Governance map (local governments, rural districts and regional service commissions), and PEI's municipal zones. If a lot falls outside every municipality, ParcelLine says so and names the district instead.
Does ParcelLine show who owns the land?
No. Owner names are not in the open parcel data. Ownership searches go through each province's land registry, which charges a fee.
How do you know if a lot is vacant?
A lot counts as undeveloped when no building outline and no civic address falls inside it. Buildings come from OpenStreetMap and, in New Brunswick, the province's building survey. Addresses come from the BC Address Geocoder and the New Brunswick and PEI civic address registries. Building maps can miss rural sheds and new construction, so treat "no buildings on record" as a lead to check on the satellite view.
Can I see how many vacant lots a town has?
In New Brunswick, yes. The province publishes every assessment account with its description, so ParcelLine counts vacant land and lists recent sales for the whole town. In BC and PEI, use "Find undeveloped lots here" to scan any neighbourhood on the map.
My condo PID says "not found". Why?
In BC the public map mostly draws whole lots. Individual strata units often are not drawn separately, so their PIDs may not appear. Try the building's street address instead.
